[0.01] NULL-TECH · CAMPUS NAV
[0.02] SUL NAV Campus navigation for Salahaddin University-Erbil
[0.03] A proposal to give every student, lecturer and visitor a simple way to find any building, room or office — on their phone, in Kurdish, Arabic or English, and even without internet.
[0.04] Prepared for Presidency, Salahaddin University-Erbil
[0.05] Prepared by NULL-TECH (project owner)
[0.06] Status Working prototype · pilot proposal · November 2026

===== 1 · Summary / پوختە =====
[1.01] 1 · Summary
[1.02] What we are asking for, in one page
[1.03] New students, visitors and even staff lose time every day looking for lecture halls, offices and services. SUL NAV is a campus navigation app that shows where things are and how to walk there, step by step, indoors and across floors.
[1.04] Find Search any room, lecturer office, department or building in Kurdish, Arabic or English.
[1.05] Walk there Turn-by-turn directions, including stairs, elevators and step-free routes.
[1.06] Stay informed Timetable, room changes, closures and announcements in one place.
[1.07] Where the project is today
[1.08] The software is built and tested: student web app (works on any phone, installable, offline), staff administration dashboard, secure database design, 2D/3D maps, real-world map, QR checkpoints, timetable and three languages. Automated tests cover the routes, security rules and the main user journeys.
[1.09] It currently runs on fictional demonstration data. No Salahaddin University buildings, rooms or timetables have been entered, because that must be done with the university's official information and permission.
[1.10] It has not yet been used by real students or tested at full university scale. This proposal includes the plan to do that safely.
[1.11] What we need from the university
[1.12] Official approval and one coordinator from the university who can open doors to the right offices.
[1.13] Campus information: official building and room lists, floor plans, and confirmation of building locations.
[1.14] Permission to put small QR signs at entrances and stairwells, and a short field survey to check every route.
[1.15] IT support: an official web address (for example nav.su.edu.krd ) and agreement on hosting and data policy.
[1.16] Proposed next step
[1.17] A pilot in one building (chosen by the university), then the whole campus once the pilot is approved. Section 5 shows the steps; section 9 lists the decisions we ask the university to make.

===== 3 · How it works =====
[3.01] 3 · How it works
[3.02] Simple for students, controlled by the university
[3.03] University staff
[3.04] Admin dashboard
[3.05] Secure database
[3.06] roles · audit log
[3.07] Campus data file
[3.08] cached worldwide (CDN)
[3.09] Student phones
[3.10] search · route · offline
[3.11] QR signs on walls
[3.12] "you are here"
[3.13] Maps and directions
[3.14] Outdoors: the real map (OpenStreetMap). Staff trace each building's outline on it, so position and scale are true to life.
[3.15] Indoors: each floor plan becomes a network of corridors, doors, stairs and elevators. The app finds the shortest valid path through that network.
[3.16] Floor plans are scaled from a measured distance (the dashboard has a calibration tool), so distances and walking times are realistic.
[3.17] Walking time is always shown as an estimate .
[3.18] Knowing where you are
[3.19] GPS does not work reliably inside concrete buildings, so the app uses QR signs : scan the sign at an entrance or stairwell and the route starts there.
[3.20] Students can also pick a place from a list or tap the floor plan.
[3.21] The app does not track students and does not need their location permission.
[3.22] Keeping data correct
[3.23] Every change by staff is recorded (who, what, when).
[3.24] Students report mistakes; staff fix them and mark them resolved.
[3.25] A verification page shows which buildings are complete and checked.

===== 5 · How the data gets installed =====
[5.01] 5 · How the data gets installed
[5.02] From approval to the whole campus
[5.03] Step 1 Set up Approval, coordinator, web address, staff accounts.
[5.04] Step 2 Pilot building Enter one building fully and place QR signs.
[5.05] Step 3 Field check Walk every route; students try it; fix reports.
[5.06] Step 4 Whole campus Repeat building by building; launch.
[5.07] Setup. The university appoints a coordinator and approves the project. IT provides the web address. We create staff accounts with the right permissions (e.g. college editors can edit only their own rooms).
[5.08] Place the campus on the real map. We confirm the campus location and trace each building's outline over the map, then the planning office checks them.
[5.09] Pilot building. We draw its floor plans in the dashboard (or import from CAD), calibrate the scale with a tape-measured distance, add rooms, doors, stairs and elevators, and print QR signs.
[5.10] Field check. Two people walk every route with the app, write down problems and fix them. Only then is the building marked "Verified".
[5.11] Student test. A small group of students uses the app for one or two weeks and sends feedback through "Report a problem".
[5.12] Campus roll-out. Repeat for every building, college by college. Timetables are imported each semester.
[5.13] Maintenance. Each college keeps its data current; closures and announcements are posted by staff; reports are reviewed weekly.
[5.14] Time
[5.15] The time depends mostly on floor-plan availability and the number of buildings. As a planning guide (to be confirmed after the pilot): setup and the pilot building take a few weeks; each further building takes days to a few weeks depending on size and plan quality. We will give a firm schedule once we know the number of buildings and the plan formats.
[5.16] Staff dashboard — verification shows what is still missing before a building can be marked "Verified". (Demonstration data.)

===== 6 · Serving all students =====
[6.01] 6 · Serving all students
[6.02] Built so that thousands of students can use it at the same time
[6.03] The busiest moment is predictable: the start of the semester and the minutes before morning lectures. The design keeps that load away from the database.
[6.04] How the load is handled
[6.05] One small data file per university. When a student opens the app, the phone downloads the campus data once. Search and route calculation then run on the phone itself — no server work per search or per route.
[6.06] Served from a CDN. That file and the app itself are cached by a content-delivery network close to the user. The database is asked at most about once every few minutes per university, no matter how many students open the app.
[6.07] Offline after first use. Returning students load from their phone's storage; the app updates in the background.
[6.08] No student accounts needed for normal use — nothing to sign in to, nothing to overload.
[6.09] Only staff edits and problem reports reach the database, and both are protected and rate-limited.
[6.10] Measured so far (lab tests)
[6.11] Route calculation on a building-sized map: well under a millisecond; a synthetic map with 14,400 points: about 29 ms.
[6.12] App start on a slow mobile connection (simulated): about 2 seconds the first time, a fraction of a second afterwards.
[6.13] Before launch we will
[6.14] Run a load test that simulates the expected peak (number of students to be agreed with the university) against the real setup.
[6.15] Test on ordinary Android phones over campus Wi-Fi.
[6.16] Set up uptime and error monitoring with alerts.
[6.17] These are laboratory measurements on test data, not results from real campus use.

===== 7 · Privacy and security =====
[7.01] 7 · Privacy and security
[7.02] Safe for students, controlled for staff
[7.03] Students
[7.04] No account, no tracking, no advertising, no analytics by default.
[7.05] No GPS or location permission. Position is only what the student chooses (QR sign or a place on the map), and it stays on their phone.
[7.06] Saved places and history stay on the student's own phone; history is off by default.
[7.07] Problem reports contain no name or contact details.
[7.08] Lecturers
[7.09] Only approved public information (name, office, office hours) is shown, with consent.
[7.10] Staff and data
[7.11] Staff sign in; roles decide who can change what (e.g. a college editor cannot change another college's rooms).
[7.12] The database enforces these rules itself, so they cannot be bypassed from the app.
[7.13] Every change is recorded in an audit log.
[7.14] Sensitive areas (server rooms, hazardous labs) can be kept off public maps — the university decides what is public.
[7.15] Security headers, rate limiting and safe handling of uploaded plans are in place and tested.
[7.16] Students can report a wrong room or a locked door without giving any personal information.
